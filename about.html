📜 Team Standards & Expectations

High standards do not mean expecting perfection. They mean taking responsibility for how we prepare, how we compete, how we treat others, and how we respond when things do not go our way.

Be Prepared

Arrive on time and ready to train or play. Bring the required equipment, take care of your body, and be mentally ready to work.

Communicate

Communicate absences or problems as early as possible. Players should increasingly learn to communicate directly with their coaches and teammates.

Train With Purpose

Be engaged, listen, ask questions, compete, and give your best effort. Mistakes are part of learning; lack of effort or attention is something we can control.

Represent the Team Well

Our behavior represents ourselves, our teammates, our families, and CE Altafulla. Treat teammates, opponents, referees, coaches, and supporters with respect.

Our Core Values

Four values will guide how we evaluate ourselves on the field, off the field, in training, and in competition.

Strength

Strength is physical, but it is also mental. Compete for the ball, protect your space, and play with courage. When we concede a goal, make a mistake, disagree with a decision, or face a difficult moment, recover and keep playing.

Discipline

Discipline means doing what the team needs from you, even when it is difficult. Understand your responsibilities, prepare properly, pay attention to details, and make decisions that help the team.

Teamwork

Football is a game of relationships. Communicate, support each other, cover for each other, share responsibility, and recognize that sometimes your best contribution is something that helps a teammate succeed.

Respect

Respect your teammates, opponents, referees, coaches, families, club, and the game itself. Compete hard without losing perspective on the responsibility and privilege of representing CE Altafulla.

Strength. Discipline. Teamwork. Respect. These standards matter when things are going well, but they matter even more when they are not.

Players & Families

As players move through the Cadete years, they should gradually take more responsibility for their own football. Players are encouraged to communicate with coaches, ask questions, solve problems, reflect on their performances, and learn to manage both success and disappointment.

Families are an important part of that process. The best support is often encouragement, perspective, and giving players enough space to find their own solutions. Coaches, players, and families will not always see every situation in exactly the same way, but communication should always remain respectful and focused on the development of the player and the team.

The goal is a demanding but positive environment in which players can compete, make mistakes, learn, and grow.

Our Football

Shared Language & Principles

The rules, principles, and standards that guide how we play, make decisions, and work together.

With the Ball

  • Ball forward → lines move up.
  • Ball backward → get wide.
  • Ball wide → 1v1.
  • Ball inside → combine.
  • Ball one side → far side tucks.
  • Ball in the air → one under, one beyond.
  • Ball in the air → don't let it bounce.
  • Can't go forward → keep it.
  • Space behind → threaten it.

Without the Ball

  • Nearest player → delay.
  • One player presses → all support.
  • When we are organized → press high.
  • We see trigger → run the trap.
  • Press broken → recover together.
  • Protect inside → force wide/back.

Transition

  • Lose it → hunt it.
  • Win it → look forward first.

In Possession

  • Create useful width and depth.
  • Recognize space before receiving.
  • Play forward when the opportunity is there.
  • Use circulation to move the opponent and create new spaces.
  • Support the player on the ball with clear passing options.

Out of Possession

  • Protect dangerous spaces first.
  • Stay connected and compact as a team.
  • Recognize when to press, delay, or recover.
  • Support the first defender with cover and communication.
  • Defend together rather than as isolated individuals.

Attacking Transition

  • Recognize immediately whether we can attack forward.
  • Exploit space before the opponent can reorganize.
  • Support the first action with forward and wide options.
  • If the counter is not available, secure possession and reorganize.

Defensive Transition

  • React immediately when possession is lost.
  • Protect the most dangerous spaces first.
  • Recognize whether we can recover the ball or need to recover our shape.
  • Communicate and solve the transition together.

These principles are not intended to create one fixed answer for every situation. The goal is to help players recognize problems, communicate, and choose solutions together.
