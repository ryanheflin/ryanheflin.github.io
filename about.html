Expectations

Team standards, values, player and family expectations, and coaching background.

Our Core Values

Four values guide how we evaluate ourselves on and off the field, in training and in matches.

Strength

Strength is physical and mental. Compete for the ball, protect your space, and play with courage. If we concede a goal, make a mistake, or face a difficult moment, we react and keep playing.

Discipline

Discipline means doing what the team needs, even when it is difficult. Know your responsibilities, prepare well, take care of the details, and make decisions that help the team.

Teamwork

Football is played together. Communicate, help, cover, share responsibility, and understand that sometimes your best action is the one that helps a teammate.

Respect

Respect teammates, opponents, referees, coaches, families, the club, and the game itself. Compete at your highest level without forgetting what it means to represent CE Altafulla.

Strength. Discipline. Teamwork. Respect. They matter when things are going well, and even more when they are not.

Team Standards & Expectations

Having high standards does not mean expecting perfection. It means taking responsibility for how we prepare, compete, treat others, and react when things do not go well.

Come Prepared

Arrive on time and prepared to train or play. Bring what you need, take care of your body, and come ready to work.

Communicate

Communicate absences or problems as early as possible. Players should gradually learn to speak directly with coaches and teammates.

Train With Purpose

Participate, listen, ask questions, compete, and give your best effort. Mistakes are part of learning; effort and attention are things we can control.

Represent the Team Well

Our behavior represents ourselves, our teammates, our families, and CE Altafulla. Treat everyone with respect.

Everyday Standards

Details Matter

Small things change the game.

Body Language Matters

Head up. Good reaction. Positive and ready for the next action.

Every Movement Has a Purpose

Understand what you are creating, closing, supporting, or threatening.

Help Your Teammate

Talk. Cover. Support. Make their job easier.

Players & Families

Helping players take increasing responsibility for their own football.

Player Responsibility

As players grow, they should take more responsibility for their football. Speak with coaches, ask questions, solve problems, reflect on their performance, and learn to manage both good and difficult moments.

Family Support

Families are an important part of the process. Often, the best support is to encourage, provide perspective, and give players space to find their own solutions.

Communication

Coaches, players, and families will not always see everything the same way, but communication should remain respectful and focused on the development of the player and the team.

The goal is to create a demanding and positive environment where players can compete, make mistakes, learn, and grow.

Game Rules

Simple cues that help us recognize situations and make decisions together.

With the Ball

  • Ball goes forward → lines move forward.
  • Ball goes back → open the field.
  • Ball goes wide → 1v1.
  • Ball goes inside → combine.
  • Ball on one side → opposite side comes inside.
  • Ball in the air → one underneath, one behind.
  • Ball in the air → do not let it bounce.
  • Cannot advance → keep the ball.
  • Space in behind → attack it.

Without the Ball

  • Nearest player → slow them down.
  • One presses → everyone helps.
  • We are organized → high press.
  • We see a trigger → activate the trap.
  • They break the press → recover together.
  • Protect inside → force outside or backward.

Transition

  • Lose it → go get it.
  • Win it → look forward first.

Playing Principles

Principles that help us recognize problems, communicate, and solve the game together.

With the Ball

  • Create useful width and depth.
  • Recognize the space before receiving.
  • Play forward when the opportunity is there.
  • Move the ball to move the opponent and create space.
  • Give clear options to the player on the ball.

Without the Ball

  • Protect dangerous spaces first.
  • Stay connected and compact.
  • Recognize when to press, delay, or recover.
  • Help the first defender with cover and communication.
  • Defend with the team, not in isolation.

Attacking Transition

  • Recognize quickly whether we can attack forward.
  • Exploit the space before the opponent can organize.
  • Support with options forward and wide.
  • If we cannot counterattack, secure the ball and organize.

Defensive Transition

  • React immediately when we lose the ball.
  • Protect the most dangerous spaces first.
  • Recognize whether we can win it back or need to recover.
  • Communicate and solve the transition together.

These principles are not meant to give a fixed answer for every situation. We want to help players recognize problems, communicate, and choose solutions together.
