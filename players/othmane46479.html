WHAT I SEE IN YOU

Your power can make you very difficult to defend.

Othmane, you bring strength, speed over distance, aggression and the confidence to attack defenders. Against San Pere, you scored twice by taking on your defender and finishing with power. That directness gives our team something important, and I don't want you to lose it.

Now I want you to add control to your power. Your first touch and passing need to become more reliable so you can play even when defenders put you under pressure. Defensively, your strength and speed can also help us, but you need to recognize situations earlier and arrive under control. We're not trying to change the player you are. We're giving you more tools to use your strengths.

WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE

IN TRAINING

  • Practice accurate 5–15 m ground passes with both feet, especially your right.
  • Receive ground and bouncing passes, moving your feet to control into a useful space.
  • In receiving drills, protect the ball from a defender and complete a short pass.
  • In 2v1 wing attacks, recognize whether to accelerate or combine.
  • In defensive games, recognize when an opponent is about to receive and start moving early.
  • Practice pressing quickly, slowing down before contact and forcing the attacker toward the sideline.

IN MATCHES

  • When receiving on the wing, make a controlled first touch before accelerating.
  • When pressed, use your strength to protect possession and find the simple supporting pass.
  • When you have space, attack with power. When the route closes, combine instead of forcing it.
  • When your opponent might receive, start closing the space before the pass arrives.
  • When pressing, approach at an angle that takes away the inside pass.
  • If your teammate presses, close the next passing option rather than attacking the same player.
