WHAT I SEE IN YOU

Your power can make you very difficult to defend.

Othmane, you bring strength, speed over distance, aggression and the confidence to attack defenders. Against San Pere, you scored twice by taking on your defender and finishing with power. That directness gives our team something important, and I don't want you to lose it.

Now I want you to add control to that power. Too often, your first touch gets away from you or your next pass isn't accurate enough. When you can control the ball, protect it and use a teammate under pressure, defenders won't be able to predict you. The goal isn't to become a different player. It's to become a more complete and dangerous version of yourself.

YOUR ATTACKING MINDSET

CONTROL IT → CHOOSE → ATTACK WITH POWER

YOUR 3 DEVELOPMENT PRIORITIES

1

CONTROL YOUR FIRST TOUCH

Move your feet before the ball arrives. Get balanced and bring the ball under control close enough to play your next action. Work especially on bouncing passes and your right foot. Don't make every first touch a chase.

2

BECOME RELIABLE ON THE BALL

Make simple, accurate passes with both feet. When a defender gets close, use your strength to protect the ball, find a teammate and move again. Keeping possession is sometimes better than trying to break through.

3

CHOOSE WHEN TO EXPLODE

Keep attacking defenders when you have space and an advantage. But don't sprint into every situation. Sometimes the best move is a controlled touch, a short pass and a new run. Change speed so your power becomes harder to stop.

YOUR DEFENSIVE HABIT

ARRIVE BALANCED, NOT LATE

See the danger early and move before the opponent gets away. Approach under control, slow the attacker and stay on your feet. You don't need to win every challenge immediately; forcing the opponent backward is useful too.

WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE

IN TRAINING

  • With a partner, repeat accurate 5–15 m ground passes with both feet. Make the right foot reliable.
  • On ground and bouncing passes, prepare your feet and control into a space you choose.
  • In a receive-and-protect drill, hold off a defender and complete a short pass to support.
  • In wing 2v1s, recognize whether to drive forward or pass and run again.
  • When defending 1v1, slow down before contact and delay the attacker without diving in.

IN MATCHES

  • When a pass arrives on the wing, keep the first touch close enough to make a choice.
  • When pressed with your back to goal, use your body and find the simple supporting pass.
  • When a defender gives you space, attack at speed. When the route closes, don't force it.
  • After a pass to a teammate, move again to receive in a better position.
  • When tracking back, arrive in balance and stop the attack rather than reaching late.
